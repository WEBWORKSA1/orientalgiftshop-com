Diwali, the festival of lights, is India's biggest gifting season. In 2027 the main day of Diwali (Lakshmi Puja) falls on **October 29**, with the five-day festival starting at Dhanteras. It is celebrated by Hindus, Jains and Sikhs across India, Nepal, Singapore, Malaysia, Mauritius, Fiji and the worldwide diaspora. Families exchange sweets and gifts, light lamps, decorate their homes and visit friends and relatives.

## Classic Diwali gifts

**Sweets (mithai).** A box of sweets is the essential Diwali gift: kaju katli, soan papdi, laddoos, barfi, gulab jamun. Buy from a good sweet shop close to the date, or choose dry sweets that keep longer if you are shipping. Include a sugar-free option for older relatives.

**Dry fruits.** Decorative boxes of almonds, cashews, pistachios, dates and raisins keep well and look generous. They are the default corporate Diwali gift.

**Diyas and candles.** Clay or brass oil lamps, decorated diya sets, scented candles and fairy lights are perfect for a festival of lights.

**Silver and gold.** Buying precious metal on **Dhanteras** is considered auspicious. Silver coins embossed with Lakshmi and Ganesha are popular gifts for family. Choose hallmarked 999 silver.

**Home décor.** Rangoli kits, torans (door hangings), brass idols, decorative urlis (water bowls with floating flowers) and cushion covers.

**Clothing.** New clothes are traditionally worn at Diwali. Kurtas, sarees, dupattas and shawls are thoughtful gifts for close family.

## Modern ideas that work

- Gourmet hampers combining sweets, chocolate, tea and savoury snacks
- Kitchen appliances or premium cookware for newlyweds and new homeowners
- Experience vouchers and spa days
- Sustainable gifts: seed-paper cards, handloom bags, terracotta planters
- Personalised gifts: engraved brass name plates, custom photo frames

## Shagun: cash gifts

Cash is often given to children and close relatives, usually in a decorated envelope. Indian tradition adds **one** to a round number, so amounts end in 1: **101, 251, 501, 1,001** and so on; larger gifts are often 1,100, 2,100 or 11,000. The extra rupee signals that the blessing is a beginning, not an end, and avoids the "finished" feeling of a round zero. Many shagun envelopes come with a coin already attached. ## What to avoid

- **Leather goods** for traditional Hindu and Jain households, because of the cow's sacred status.
- **Alcohol,** unless you know the family drinks. Many do not, and Diwali is a religious occasion.
- **Non-vegetarian food**, and beef in particular. Many families are vegetarian, at least during festivals. Jain households also avoid root vegetables, and some Hindu families avoid onion and garlic during religious periods.
- **Black wrapping or clothing,** which is considered inauspicious for celebrations.
- **Sharp objects** such as knives and scissors in some families.
- **Used or second-hand items,** unless they are genuine heirlooms.

## Diwali gifts for clients and staff

Diwali is the main corporate gifting moment in India, much like year-end in the West. Popular choices are dry-fruit boxes, sweets and chocolate hampers, brass or silver décor, branded tech accessories and gift cards. Keep gifts vegetarian and alcohol-free by default. ## How to give

Visit with your gift in the days around Diwali, ideally before the main day. Greet with **Shubh Deepavali** or **Happy Diwali**. Gifts are usually offered with the right hand or both hands. If you are visiting during puja (prayer) time, wait until it is finished. Recipients often return the favour with sweets, so expect to leave with a box too.

## Bhai Dooj

The fifth day of Diwali, **Bhai Dooj**, celebrates the bond between brothers and sisters. Sisters perform a tilak ceremony for their brothers, who give gifts in return: jewellery, cosmetics, gadgets, clothing or cash.
