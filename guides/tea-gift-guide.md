Tea is the most universally welcome gift across East and South Asia. It signals respect, health and good taste; it is consumable, so it never clutters; and it can be as modest or as luxurious as the occasion needs. Here is how to choose well.

## What each tea says

| Tea | Origin | Character | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Longjing (Dragon Well) | China | Toasty, chestnut green tea | Respectful gift for elders and connoisseurs |
| Oolong (Tieguanyin, Da Hong Pao, Alishan) | China, Taiwan | Floral to roasted, many infusions | Business gifts, tea lovers |
| Pu-erh | Yunnan, China | Earthy, ages like wine | Collectors, prosperity gift (compressed cakes) |
| Jasmine pearls | Fujian, China | Fragrant, gentle | Beginners, friends, hosts |
| Matcha | Japan | Grassy, umami, whisked | Wellness lovers, Japan fans |
| Gyokuro and sencha | Japan | Sweet, rich green | Japanese recipients, refined palates |
| Darjeeling and Assam | India | Muscatel to malty black tea | Indian recipients, Western tea drinkers |
| Korean nokcha, barley or yuja tea | Korea | Light, nutty or citrus-honey | Korean hosts, families |

## How to pick quality

- **Choose one excellent tea over a sampler of average ones.** A named origin and harvest (for example "spring 2026 Longjing") reads as thoughtful.
- **Loose leaf beats teabags** for gifts.
- **Check packaging.** Airtight tins or sealed foil with a harvest or packing date.
- **Buy from specialists.** Tea shops that state origin, grade and farm or producer are more reliable than generic gift brands.
- **For matcha,** look for "ceremonial grade", a vivid green colour and a recent packing date. Store it in the fridge once opened.

## Pairing with teaware

A tea and teaware set makes a complete, memorable gift:

- **Gaiwan** (lidded bowl) with oolong for a Chinese tea lover
- **Kyusu** (side-handled teapot) with sencha for a Japanese tea gift
- **Matcha bowl (chawan), bamboo whisk (chasen) and scoop (chashaku)** for a starter kit
- **Glass teapot** with blooming jasmine for beginners

Mind the number of cups: two, six or eight are good; **never four**.

## Tea for occasions

- **Lunar New Year:** premium oolong or aged pu-erh in red and gold packaging.
- **Mid-Autumn:** tea to pair with mooncakes; oolong and pu-erh cut through the richness.
- **Business:** an elegant tin of a named regional tea with a handwritten card.
- **Weddings:** a tea set for two; tea is served to parents in Chinese wedding tea ceremonies.
- **Thank-you:** a small tin of jasmine or sencha.

One caution in Japan: green tea is also a common **funeral return gift**, so for celebrations choose festive packaging or pair it with sweets.

## How to present it

Wrap in a colour suited to the occasion, give with both hands, and add a short note on how to brew it (water temperature and steep time). Recipients new to a tea will appreciate the guidance as much as the tea itself.
