Across most of Asia, the standard wedding gift is **cash in a special envelope**, not an item from a registry. That can feel strange to Western guests, but it is practical and deeply traditional: cash helps the couple pay for the celebration and start their household. Here is how each culture does it.

## Chinese weddings

Guests give a **red envelope (hongbao)** with an **even amount** that avoids 4. A common rule of thumb is to cover at least the estimated cost of your seat at the banquet, more if you are close family. Write a short blessing and your name on the envelope; there is often a reception table where envelopes are collected and recorded. Popular wishes include **百年好合** (a harmonious union for a hundred years) and **永结同心** (forever united in heart).

If you prefer to give an item, choose something in pairs: two cups, a pair of chopstick sets, matching bowls, or bedding in red. Avoid clocks, pears, umbrellas, knives and anything white or black.

## Japanese weddings

Japanese weddings are invitation-only, and attending guests give **goshugi**: **30,000 yen** is the standard from friends and colleagues, in new notes, inside a shūgi-bukuro envelope with a gold and silver or red and white mizuhiki knot that cannot be untied. Avoid even leading numbers (which can be "split") and especially 4 and 9. Couples give guests a **hikidemono** return gift: often catalogue gifts, sweets or tableware.

## Korean weddings

Guests hand **chukuigeum** in a white envelope at the reception desk, where it is logged. Typical amounts are **50,000 won** for acquaintances and **100,000 won or more** for close friends or upscale venues, always in odd units. Close friends sometimes also give household items. Avoid shoes for the couple.

## Indian weddings

Cash **shagun** is common, in amounts ending in 1 (501, 1,001, 2,001…, or 1,100 and 2,100 for larger gifts). Close family give jewellery, gold or silver, clothing, and household items. Registries are increasingly common in urban and diaspora weddings. Avoid leather and black; many families also prefer no alcohol as a gift.

## Vietnamese weddings

Guests bring cash in an envelope (**phong bì**) handed to the couple or placed in a box at the reception. Amounts track the venue and closeness. Red and gold are lucky; avoid sets of four.

## Thai weddings

Cash in a white or decorated envelope is usual, often handed in at the reception table. Gold jewellery is a traditional gift from close family. The couple may give small favours such as fans or jasmine-scented soap.

## Item gifts couples love

- Premium tea set for two
- Paired porcelain bowls or chopsticks
- High-end rice cooker or kitchen appliance (check voltage)
- Silk bedding or a quality duvet in a festive colour
- Lacquerware jewellery box
- Framed calligraphy of a wedding blessing
