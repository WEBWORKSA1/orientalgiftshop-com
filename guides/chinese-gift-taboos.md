Most Chinese gift taboos come from **homophones**, words that sound like something unlucky. Chinese has many words that share similar sounds, so a gift can accidentally "say" something you never meant. Others come from symbolism around funerals and relationships. Younger and overseas-raised Chinese people may not mind many of these, but elders and traditional families often do, especially at Lunar New Year, weddings and birthdays.

## The big five

**1. Clocks (钟 zhōng).** "Giving a clock" (送钟 sòng zhōng) sounds exactly like "attending a dying person's final moments" (送终). This is the most widely known taboo. *Instead:* a quality tea set or a desk ornament.

**2. Pears (梨 lí).** Sounds like "leaving" or "separation" (离). Sharing a pear (分梨) sounds like parting (分离). *Instead:* apples (苹果, sounding like peace, 平安) or mandarin oranges.

**3. Umbrellas (伞 sǎn).** Sounds like "to break up" (散). Especially bad for couples and friends. *Instead:* a silk scarf.

**4. The number four (四 sì).** Sounds like "death" (死). Avoid sets of four, prices with a 4 and amounts like 40 or 400 in red envelopes. *Instead:* sets of two, six or eight.

**5. Sharp objects.** Knives, scissors and letter openers symbolise cutting the relationship. If someone gives you one, offering a coin in return turns it into a "purchase". *Instead:* chopsticks, which come in pairs.

## Ten more to know

| Gift | Why it is avoided | Better choice |
|---|---|---|
| Shoes (鞋 xié) | Sounds like "evil" (邪) in some dialects; suggests walking away | Slippers only for family who ask; otherwise a scarf |
| Green hat | "Wearing a green hat" means a man's partner is unfaithful | Any other colour |
| White or black wrapping | Mourning colours | Red, gold, pink |
| White flowers, chrysanthemums | Funeral flowers | Peonies, orchids, lilies in colour |
| Mirrors | Thought to attract spirits; fragile like a relationship | Jade or a framed calligraphy blessing |
| Books for business owners | "Book" (书) sounds like "lose" (输) | A fine pen set |
| Handkerchiefs | Associated with tears and goodbyes | A silk scarf |
| Candles (traditional households) | Associated with memorial rites | Lucky bamboo |
| Empty wallets | Suggests poverty | Put a coin or note inside |
| Cheap copies of luxury goods | Loss of face for giver and receiver | A genuine, smaller item |

## Lucky alternatives

Some sounds are lucky too. **Eight (八 bā)** sounds like "prosper" (发). **Fish (鱼 yú)** sounds like "surplus" (余). **Mandarins** sound like gold and luck. **Apples** suggest peace. **Peaches** stand for longevity at birthdays. **Pairs** reflect the saying "good things come in pairs" (好事成双), so even numbers are preferred for happy occasions.

## Colours

Red is the colour of joy and luck; gold signals wealth. Pink and yellow are positive. White and black belong to funerals. Never write someone's name in red ink, which was historically used for the names of the deceased.

## Etiquette when giving

- Present the gift with **both hands**.
- Expect the recipient to **decline politely once or twice** before accepting; offer again.
- The gift is usually **opened later**, not in front of you.
- Downplay the gift; avoid mentioning the price.
- In business, give the gift privately to the senior person or as a group gift, and check the recipient's company or government gift rules.
