The Mid-Autumn Festival (中秋节) falls on the 15th day of the eighth lunar month, when the moon is said to be at its fullest. In 2027 it is on **Wednesday, September 15**. It is a festival of family reunion, celebrated in China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia and Vietnam (as Tết Trung Thu), and on the same date Korea celebrates Chuseok.

## Mooncakes: the essential gift

Round like the full moon, mooncakes symbolise completeness and togetherness. Exchanging boxes with family, friends and business partners is the heart of the festival.

**Main styles:**
- **Cantonese baked:** thin golden crust, filled with lotus seed paste, often with salted egg yolks (one yolk = one moon; two or more for luxury).
- **Snow skin:** chilled, mochi-like skin with modern fillings such as durian, matcha or custard. Must be kept cold.
- **Suzhou style:** flaky pastry, sweet or savoury (minced pork) fillings.
- **Custard and lava:** a Hong Kong favourite.
- **Teochew and Yunnan:** flaky, or filled with ham.

**Choosing a box:**
- Well-known hotel and bakery brands are standard business gifts; boxes are part of the appeal.
- Avoid **boxes of four**; standard boxes of two, six or eight are safer.
- For health-conscious elders, choose low-sugar or single-yolk options.
- Order early: popular boxes sell out and early-bird discounts are common.

## Beyond mooncakes

- **Tea** to cut through rich mooncakes: oolong, pu-erh or chrysanthemum (fine as a tea).
- **Pomelos,** in season and round, symbolising family unity.
- **Lanterns** for children, from classic paper to light-up character lanterns.
- **Fruit baskets** with persimmons, grapes and apples; skip pears for Chinese recipients.
- **Osmanthus wine or sweets,** associated with the moon legend.

## Etiquette

- Give mooncakes in the two weeks before the festival.
- Visiting family on the night itself is traditional; bring a box and fruit.
- For clients, a branded sleeve or card is common; keep the box itself elegant.
- A good greeting: **中秋节快乐** (Zhōngqiū jié kuàilè, Happy Mid-Autumn Festival), or **花好月圆** (blooming flowers and a full moon). See the [Greetings Phrasebook](greetings.html).
