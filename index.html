The five rules that work almost everywhere

  1. Give and receive with both hands. Standard courtesy in East and Southeast Asia.
  2. Skip the number four. It sounds like "death" in Chinese, Japanese and Korean.
  3. Wrap it, and not in plain white or black. Presentation is part of the gift.
  4. Edible beats clutter. Quality food, tea and fruit are welcome almost everywhere.
  5. Don't expect it opened in front of you. Many recipients open gifts later, in private.

Questions people ask

Is OrientalGiftShop.com a store?

No. We are an independent guide and toolset. We do not sell or ship products. Where we suggest where to look for an item, the links go to third-party marketplaces, and some may be affiliate links that support the site at no cost to you.

Why the name "Oriental"?

"Oriental gift shop" is a long-standing term for shops selling Asian décor, tea and crafts, and it is how many people still search. We use it only for objects and shops, never to describe people, and our guides name each culture specifically.

What is the safest gift if I know nothing about the recipient?

A good-quality edible gift, such as tea, premium fruit or a box of sweets, wrapped neatly in a colour other than plain white or black, in a quantity that is not four. It works across China, Japan, Korea, India and Southeast Asia.

How much should I put in a red envelope?

It depends on your relationship and local norms. Use an even amount without the number 4 for Chinese recipients, new notes, and give with both hands. Our Lucky Amount Calculator suggests amounts for Chinese, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese and Indian customs.
